How the noise estimate works

Explore the combined noise from your machines. These are estimates, not site measurements.

Machine power and receiver levels

LwA is the machine's sound power. Enter the value from its equipment data when available. Starter values are examples.

LpA is the estimated pressure level at a receiver. Place a receiver where you want to check the combined machine noise.

Sound spreads with distance

In open space, doubling the distance reduces one machine's level by about 6 dB.

Calculation details

LpA = LwA − 20 log10(distance in metres) − 10 log10(4π)

Power is referenced to 1 picowatt and pressure to 20 micropascals. Distances below 1 m use a numerical limit.

Several machines combine

Two independent 60 dB(A) contributions at a receiver combine to about 63 dB(A). Contours join locations with the same combined level.

Understand the limits

The calculation currently assumes open space. Terrain, barriers, ground and atmospheric effects, and operating cycles are not yet included. Recorded cloud cover does not change the result.

A calculated level is not evidence that a machine can be heard above local background noise. Check important results against site data.

Distance and negative values

There is no fixed receiver-distance cutoff. Contours may fail if the map projection or calculation budget cannot represent the complete line; other levels remain available.

Decibels can be negative because zero dB is a reference level, not silence. A negative value is a small predicted contribution.

Background and the outer boundary

Enter one background noise level for your scenario. Use a measurement or record your assumption; the app does not choose a background value for you.

The dotted Negligible overall increase contour marks where all machines together add 0.1 dB to background. Beyond it, receivers show Negligible overall increase and lower contours are hidden. The boundary moves when machines or background change.

Other contours and numeric receiver readings show the machinery contribution, excluding background. A negligible broadband increase does not guarantee that a distinctive tone cannot be heard. Compare compatible A-weighted measurements and averaging periods.

How background is combined

Increase = 10 log10(1 + 10^((machinery − background) / 10))

The outer contour is about 16.3 dB below background. This is a display setting, not a legal limit or a propagation correction. With background blank, the filter is off. Extreme powers such as 300 dB remain experimental arithmetic, outside ordinary machinery modelling.

Keep your scenario

Save keeps a named copy on the computer running the app. Open returns to it.

If unsaved edits are found, choose Restore to recover the browser draft. Recovery does not update named saves automatically and is not a backup.

Address search

Address and place suggestions come from Photon using OpenStreetMap data. Search text and the map centre used to prioritise nearby matches are sent to Photon through this app's backend. Machine locations, receiver locations and sound inputs are not sent.

Search needs an internet connection. If no result is available, use Go to coordinates. Choosing a location moves the map; it does not move your machines or receivers.
